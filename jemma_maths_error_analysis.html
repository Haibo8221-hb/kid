Q3. Standard Form Calculation LOW

(5 × 10⁴) × (3 × 10²)   [2 marks]   Scored: 1/2

What Jemma Wrote

15 × 10⁶

Correct Answer

1.5 × 10⁷

Root Cause

Jemma correctly multiplied coefficients (5 × 3 = 15) and added exponents (4 + 2 = 6) — these are the right steps. But she forgot the final step: standard form requires the coefficient to be between 1 and 10. 15 is not — it must be written as 1.5, which pushes the exponent up by one (from 6 to 7).

This is a format error, not a calculation error. She knows how to multiply in scientific notation; she just stopped one step too early.

Full Working

(5 × 10⁴) × (3 × 10²) = 15 × 10⁶ 15 = 1.5 × 10¹, so: 15 × 10⁶ = 1.5 × 10¹ × 10⁶ = 1.5 × 10⁷

Drill

Always check: "Is the first number between 1 and 10?" If not, adjust. Practice 5 questions: (6×10³)(2×10⁵), (4×10²)(5×10¹), (8×10⁴)(3×10³), (7×10²)(4×10⁵), (9×10¹)(6×10³) — for each, give the answer in correct standard form.

Q4. Mixed Numbers: Addition & Subtraction HIGH

2⅔ + 1⅚ and 3⅕ − 1¾   [3 marks]   Scored: 1/3

What Jemma Wrote

(a) 2⅔ + 1⅚ — attempted but incorrect conversion (b) 3⅕ − 1¾ — subtraction error; likely didn't find correct common denominator

Correct Working

(a) 2⅔ + 1⅚ = 8/3 + 11/6 = 16/6 + 11/6 = 27/6 = 9/2 = 4½ (b) 3⅕ − 1¾ = 16/5 − 7/4 = 64/20 − 35/20 = 29/20 = 1⁹⁄₂₀

Root Cause

Jemma's mixed number operations are mechanically shaky. She may know the general idea (convert to improper, find common denominator, add/subtract) but makes errors in one or more of the sub-steps — especially choosing the common denominator. For ⅔ + ⅚, the LCD is 6, not 18. For ⅕ − ¾, the LCD is 20.

This is a skill gap, not a knowledge gap. She needs repetitive drill to make the steps automatic.

The 4-Step Method for Mixed Number Operations

Step 1: Convert ALL mixed numbers to improper fractions 2⅔ → (2×3+2)/3 = 8/3 1⅚ → (1×6+5)/6 = 11/6 Step 2: Find the LCM of the denominators 3 and 6 → LCM = 6 Step 3: Convert both fractions to the common denominator 8/3 = 16/6 11/6 stays 11/6 Step 4: Add/subtract, then simplify 16/6 + 11/6 = 27/6 = 9/2 = 4½

Drill — 10 Questions, Every Day for 1 Week

Generate 10 random mixed-number addition/subtraction problems daily. Use Corbettmaths Fractions worksheets or MathsBot generator. Aim for 90%+ accuracy before moving to multiplication/division.

Q5. Mixed Numbers: Multiplication & Division HIGH

3¼ × 2⅖ and 4½ ÷ 1⅛   [3 marks]   Scored: 0/3

What Jemma Wrote

(a) 3¼ × 2⅖ — attempted, likely multiplied whole parts and fractions separately (a common mistake) (b) 4½ ÷ 1⅛ — division error; possibly kept as division instead of multiplying by reciprocal

Correct Working

(a) 3¼ × 2⅖ = 13/4 × 12/5 = 156/20 = 39/5 = 7⅘ (b) 4½ ÷ 1⅛ = 9/2 ÷ 9/8 = 9/2 × 8/9 = 72/18 = 4

Root Cause

Two likely errors:

Multiplication: Jemma may have tried to multiply whole parts (3 × 2 = 6) and fractions (¼ × ⅖ = ²⁄₂₀) separately, then added them. This does NOT work — the whole and fraction are part of the same number. Must convert to improper first.

Division: She may have kept 9/2 ÷ 9/8 without flipping to multiply by the reciprocal. The phrase "Keep-Change-Flip" helps: Keep the first fraction, Change ÷ to ×, Flip the second fraction.

Drill

Multiplication: 2½×1⅓, 4⅔×3½, 5¼×2⅖, 1⅞×3⅓, 6⅔×1½ — convert ALL to improper first.
Division (KCF): 3½÷1¼, 5⅓÷2⅔, 4⅕÷1⅖, 6¼÷2½, 2⅞÷1¾ — use Keep-Change-Flip.
Resources: Corbettmaths Multiplying Fractions | Dividing Fractions

1¾ cups for 1 cake → how many for 2½ cakes?   [2 marks]   Scored: 0/2

What Jemma Wrote

Attempted but unclear working — likely added instead of multiplying, or tried to multiply whole and fraction parts separately

Correct Solution

1¾ × 2½ = 7/4 × 5/2 = 35/8 = 4⅜ cups

Root Cause

This is the same mixed-number multiplication gap as Q5, but disguised as a word problem. The extra step of recognising it's a multiplication problem may have added confusion. Recipe scaling problems always use multiplication when finding the amount for multiple batches.

Q7. Fractions, Decimals & Percentages Table MEDIUM

Complete ⅗ → decimal → %; 0.35 → fraction → %; 62.5% → fraction → decimal   [3 marks]   Scored: 2/3

What Jemma Wrote

One cell incorrect — likely 62.5% row (fraction or decimal)

Correct Table

⅗ → 0.6 → 60% 0.35 → 7/20 → 35% 62.5% → 5/8 → 0.625

Root Cause

62.5% is a non-obvious percentage. The decimal is 0.625, and the fraction is 5/8. If Jemma doesn't have the ⅛-family equivalents memorised (⅛=12.5%, ⅜=37.5%, ⅝=62.5%, ⅞=87.5%), she'd need to do 62.5÷100=0.625 then simplify 625/1000=5/8 — which is error-prone under time pressure.

Drill — Memorise the Eighths

⅛ = 0.125 = 12.5%
¼ = 0.25 = 25%
⅜ = 0.375 = 37.5%
½ = 0.5 = 50%
⅝ = 0.625 = 62.5%
¾ = 0.75 = 75%
⅞ = 0.875 = 87.5%

These come up constantly in GCSE. Worth memorising cold.

Q11. Compound Interest — 3 Years MEDIUM

£2500 at 4% compound for 3 years   [3 marks]   Scored: 2/3

What Jemma Wrote

2500 × 1.04² — used power of 2 instead of 3

Correct Working

2500 × 1.04³ = 2500 × (1.04 × 1.04 × 1.04) = 2500 × 1.124864 = £2812.16

Root Cause

Jemma used the correct formula (multiplier = 1 + rate/100, then raise to power of years). The method was right. But she raised to power 2 instead of 3 — a careless slip, likely from reading "3 years" and then writing exponent 2 by mistake. The working was on the right track.

Drill — Double-Check the Exponent

After writing the formula, always re-read the question and count: "Year 1, Year 2, Year 3 — that's 3 times." Practice: £800 at 5% for 4 years; £1500 at 3% for 2 years; £5000 at 2.5% for 5 years. For each, write out the multiplier and exponent, then calculate.

Q34. Circumference with Unit Conversion MEDIUM

Bicycle wheel diameter 70 cm → turns in 1 km   [3 marks]   Scored: 1/3

What Jemma Wrote

C = πd = 3.14 × 70 = 219.8 cm — but then used this directly with 1 km without converting units

Correct Working

C = πd = 3.14 × 70 = 219.8 cm 1 km = 1000 m = 100 000 cm Number of turns = 100 000 ÷ 219.8 = 454.96... = 454 complete turns

Root Cause

Jemma correctly calculated the circumference in cm. But she then tried to divide 1 by 219.8 (treating 1 as 1 km directly) without converting km to cm first. This is a unit consistency error — mixing km and cm in the same division without converting both to the same unit.

Golden rule: before dividing, ALL units must match. 1 km = 100 000 cm.

Drill — Unit Conversion Ladder

km → m → cm → mm: each step is ×1000, ×100, ×10.
Practice: A wheel of radius 35 cm. How many turns in 2 km?
A wheel of diameter 50 cm. How many turns in 500 m?
Always write: "Convert everything to the SAME unit first."

Q38. Describing a Single Transformation LOW

Enlargement ×2 from (0,0) then translation (−3,1) → single transformation   [3 marks]   Scored: 2/3

What Jemma Wrote

(a) P(2,3) → (4,6) after enlargement —— CORRECT (b) (4,6) → (1,7) after translation —— CORRECT (c) Did not describe the SINGLE transformation

Full Answer

Step 1: Enlargement ×2 from (0,0): (2,3)→(4,6) ✓ Step 2: Translation (−3,1): (4,6)→(1,7) ✓ Step 3: The single transformation: Enlargement by scale factor 2 from centre (−3,1)

Root Cause

Jemma executed both transformations correctly — she got the final coordinate (1,7) right. But the question asked for a single transformation that maps P directly to the final point. When you combine an enlargement from (0,0) + translation, the result is an enlargement from a different centre. Finding that centre requires working backwards: translate the image point back by the inverse of the translation, then find the centre that maps the original to the back-translated point.

This is a method gap — she doesn't know how to find the single equivalent transformation for a combination. This is a Grade 8–9 topic, so losing 1 mark here is expected.
