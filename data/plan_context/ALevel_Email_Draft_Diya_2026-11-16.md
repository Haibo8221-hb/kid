**About Politics and Business - honest comparison, clear conclusion**

Both are real subjects with real value, so this isn't a dismissal:

- **Politics** is a proper essay subject, respected by universities like LSE (it sits on their "generally preferred" list). If you genuinely enjoy following current affairs, that curiosity is an asset - keep reading, keep arguing at the dinner table. But Politics means weekly essays, and in Year 12 your autumn term belongs to ESAT practice, the SMC and competitions. A fourth subject that takes hours from Maths and Physics without adding anything to an engineering application is a trade you don't need to make. If the interest stays alive, Headington's Electives programme in Year 12 lets you pick politics-flavoured courses at zero cost to your A-levels.
- **Business** reads easily and feels practical, but it's on LSE's official "non-preferred" list and adds nothing to an engineering personal statement. If business curiosity strikes, the school's Mini MBA and Electives cover it - again, without spending an A-level slot.
- **Chemistry** was the old backup plan, but here's the surprise: Imperial's engineering test doesn't use Chemistry at all (only Chemical Engineering does), and offers don't reward it either. Which means your GCSE Chemistry no longer needs to be forced to a 7 for subject-choice reasons - take it at whatever grade reflects your effort, and spend the saved energy on Maths and Physics.

So the conclusion isn't "Politics or Business or Chemistry" - it's that the best fourth subject is none of them, and the time goes back to the three that Imperial actually counts.

**The EPQ - optional, and not a decision for today**

Headington offers the EPQ: a half-A-level research project done inside Year 12. It won't lower an Imperial offer (they don't do that), and HKU doesn't count it as a subject. Its real value for you would be the 5,000-word long-form writing practice and a project you genuinely care about - useful things, if you have the bandwidth. My suggestion: don't sign up now. Next September, try it for a few weeks; if a project idea truly grabs you and three hours a week feels sustainable, commit then. If not, walk away guilt-free.

**The one thing that will actually decide your Imperial odds: the ESAT**

It's in October 2028 (start of Year 13). Sounds far away, so here's the honest map:

- Now until GCSEs: nothing extra. Just keep GCSEs at grade 7+ in your A-level subjects (that's the entry ticket) and let your calculator-free arithmetic get sharp through normal Maths practice - ESAT gives you ~90 seconds per question, no calculator.
- Summer after GCSEs: read the official ESAT specification and mark the Maths 2 topics school hasn't taught yet (calculus, logarithms, radians). That's your head-start list - the only thing worth getting ahead on.
- Year 12: your SMC and SPC competitions double as speed training for exactly the ESAT style. Weekly timed practice from spring.
- Summer of Year 12: the main push - weekly full mock sittings. That's when the real preparation happens, not before.

You'll notice nothing on this list requires you to sacrifice Year 11 or start cramming early. The plan is deliberately front-loaded onto your GCSEs and back-loaded onto Year 12 summer, where it belongs.

**This term, just three things:**
