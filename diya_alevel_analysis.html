6. A-Level History — What It Actually Looks Like

Based on AQA 7042 Specification (2015). If Diya considers History as her 4th A-level instead of Chemistry.

Three Components

ComponentWhatExamWeight
1: Breadth Study ~100 years of historical change. Focus on themes and interpretations. 2h 30m written
3 questions (1 compulsory)		 40%
2: Depth Study A period of major change. Primary source analysis. 2h 30m written
3 questions (1 compulsory)		 40%
3: Personal Investigation Your own research project. Any topic you choose. 3,500–4,500 word essay. No exam — marked by teacher, moderated by AQA 20%

What a Real Exam Question Looks Like

Component 1 — Section A (Compulsory, 30 marks)

Using your understanding of the historical context, assess how convincing the arguments in Extract A, Extract B, and Extract C are in relation to [a specific historical debate, e.g. "the reasons for the outbreak of the English Civil War in 1642"].

You get three short extracts from historians arguing different positions. You must evaluate each one — not summarise, but judge: what evidence supports it? what evidence contradicts it? how convincing is the argument?

Component 1 — Section B (Choose 2 of 3, 25 marks each)

"The personalities of rulers were the most important factor in shaping political change in Britain in the years 1625 to 1688."

Assess the validity of this view.

This is the classic A-level History essay. You must argue for AND against, weigh evidence, reach a balanced conclusion. ~45 minutes per essay.

The NEA: Your Own Research Project

The Personal Investigation is 3,500–4,500 words on a topic you choose. It must cover about 100 years, and must not overlap with your exam topics.

Example topics students have done: "To what extent was Churchill's leadership the decisive factor in British victory in WWII?" or "How far did the Cold War originate from misunderstandings rather than deliberate aggression?"

The NEA is 20% of your A-level — and it's done over one year, not in a single exam. You research, draft, get feedback, revise. It's the closest thing to university-style work in any A-level.
