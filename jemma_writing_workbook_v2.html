The Outsider's Diary

★★ Medium

Jemma, your story's narrator is an exile — this is your strength. Write a diary entry (200 words) from the perspective of someone who doesn't belong. Choose one:

  • A mermaid who has just grown legs and is walking on land for the first time
  • A ghost watching their own family eat dinner without them
  • The last person on Earth who remembers how to read
  • A dragon disguised as a human, attending a normal school

Must Include

  • At least two sensory details that show their difference
  • One moment of longing for their original world
  • One moment of unexpected joy in the new world
  • A closing line that leaves the reader thinking

The Unreliable Narrator

★★ Medium

Write a 150-word scene from a narrator who is not telling the whole truth. The reader should gradually realise something is wrong.

Ideas:
• A character describing a "perfect" family dinner — but the details keep slipping
• Someone explaining why they're late — each sentence makes it less believable
• A diary entry that contradicts itself between the first and last paragraph

11

Write a Complete Flash Fiction (300 words)

★★★ Challenging

Write a complete story in exactly 300 words with a beginning, middle, and end. Choose one prompt:

  • "The door had never been there before."
  • "She was the only one who noticed the colour had changed."
  • "The message said 'don't open it' — so of course, she did."

Must Include

  • A clear change between the beginning and end
  • At least one moment of tension
  • A satisfying final sentence
12

Constraint Writing

★★★ Challenging

Write a 150-word scene with strict constraints. Constraints force creativity.

Scene: A character discovers a hidden door.

Constraints:
• No adverbs ending in -ly
• No form of the verb "to be" (is, was, were, are, am, been, being)
• Must include one colour and one sound
Removing "to be" verbs forces active, vivid sentences. Instead of "The door was old", write "The door sagged on its hinges" or "Centuries had warped the oak."
📖 The PEEL FrameworkFoundation

How to Write a PEEL Analysis Paragraph

PEEL is your formula for explaining how a writer creates an effect — not just what happens. Use it for any text: novels, short stories, poems, articles.

P — Point

One clear sentence stating your argument. What effect does the writer create?

"Rowling creates a sense of wonder and unfamiliarity as Harry enters Diagon Alley for the first time."

E — Evidence

A short, embedded quotation that supports your point. Embed means weave it into your sentence.

The description of Harry "wishing he had about eight more eyes" to take in everything conveys...

E — Explain

Name the writer's technique and explain its effect. What does it make the reader think or feel? Pick specific words and explore their connotations.

The hyperbole of "eight more eyes" emphasises the overwhelming sensory overload. The verb "wishing" suggests a childlike eagerness — Harry isn't just observing; he's desperate to absorb every detail, which makes the reader share his excitement.

L — Link

Connect back to the wider text, the writer's purpose, or the reader's response. Why does this matter?

This moment of wonder is important because it marks Harry's first real taste of the magical world — and the reader discovers it alongside him, building our sympathy for his journey from the ordinary into the extraordinary.

Technique Glossary

TechniqueDefinitionExample from Modern Texts
SimileComparison using "like" or "as""The lake was as smooth as dark glass" — Holes, Louis Sachar
MetaphorDirect comparison (says it IS something else)"Memories are bullets" — The Graveyard Book, Neil Gaiman
PersonificationGiving human qualities to non-human things"The house seemed to be holding its breath" — Coraline, Neil Gaiman
Sensory imageryDescription that appeals to the five senses"The smell of chocolate and fresh-baked bread drifted through the train window" — Harry Potter, J.K. Rowling
Pathetic fallacyWeather/setting reflecting characters' emotions"The rain hammered against the windows, matching August's frustration" — Wonder, R.J. Palacio
Short sentenceA brief sentence for emphasis or shock"He stopped. Listened. Nothing." — Ghost, Jason Reynolds
Adjective choiceCarefully chosen describing words"the brittle silence" — suggests silence that could shatter, tension underneath
Verb choiceSpecific action words that create effect"The wind clawed at the tent" — wind as predator, not just weather
JuxtapositionPlacing contrasting things side by sideAuggie's first day at school: excitement vs. stares; hope vs. cruelty — Wonder
RepetitionRepeating a word or phrase for emphasis"I am not strange. I am not a monster. I am not a mistake." — Wonder
First-person narration"I" voice — limited to one character's view"There was a hand in the darkness, and it held a knife." — The Graveyard Book
ForeshadowingHinting at what will happen laterThe mysterious locked door that characters keep mentioning but never open
SymbolismAn object representing a bigger ideaStanley's empty canteen in Holes — thirst representing the search for truth

📝 PEEL Sentence Starters

Print this and keep it next to you when writing analysis paragraphs.

PartSentence Starters
Point"The writer presents...", "Through the use of...", "The writer creates a sense of...", "The author establishes..."
Evidence"This is evident when...", "The description of '...' conveys...", "The phrase '...' suggests...", "The writer's choice of '...' implies..."
Explain"The [technique] creates the effect of...", "This evokes a feeling of...", "By using [technique], the writer emphasises...", "The word '...' is particularly significant because its connotations include...", "On a deeper level, '...' could also suggest..."
