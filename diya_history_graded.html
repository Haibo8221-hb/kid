Section B · Question 3(a) (8 marks — Source Utility)

The Sources

Source B: From Inside the Third Reich, the autobiography of Albert Speer, published in 1970. Here Speer is recalling election rallies on 27 July 1932. At the time, he was a junior member of the Nazi Party. Speer later became a senior member of the Nazi government.

"Our cars set out for the first stadium. I waited outside so I did not hear Hitler's speech, but I could hear thunderous applause from the crowd. ... At the Berlin Stadium the stands were packed with people and more were waiting outside. Hitler was very late but the crowd had still waited for hours. A roar of applause burst out when they heard Hitler was on his way."

Source C: From the diary of Joachim von Ribbentrop, written in 1933. Ribbentrop was a wealthy businessman and supporter of the Nazi Party.

"27 Jan: I eventually convince von Papen that the only thing that makes sense is for Hindenburg to appoint Hitler as Chancellor. Von Papen is now absolutely in favour. I believe this is the turning point. 28 Jan: Von Papen says he has had a long talk with Hindenburg. It is now possible Hindenburg could agree. 30 Jan: Hitler is appointed Chancellor."

Question

How useful are Sources B and C for an enquiry into the reasons why Hitler became Chancellor in 1933? Explain your answer, using Sources B and C and your knowledge of the historical context. [8 marks]

What's Missing — The Gap to Level 3 (7-8 marks)

Source B — Speer's Autobiography

Nature Autobiography/memoir

Origin Albert Speer, published 1970 (38 years after events)

Purpose Written to rehabilitate his image after serving 20 years for war crimes at Nuremberg. Speer portrayed himself as an "apolitical technocrat" — this memoir is part of that self-defence.

✅ Useful: insider, witnessed events
⚠️ Limited: retrospective, self-serving, written to distance himself from Nazi crimes

Source C — Ribbentrop's Diary

Nature Personal diary

Origin Ribbentrop, wealthy Nazi supporter, Jan 1933 (contemporary)

Purpose Personal record; but Ribbentrop had a vested interest in presenting Hitler's rise as inevitable and his own role as crucial. He later became Foreign Minister — the diary may exaggerate his influence.

✅ Useful: contemporary, shows backroom politics
⚠️ Limited: Nazi supporter, may inflate own importance

Diya vs Level 3 — Side by Side

🔸 Diya (Level 2+ · 6/8)

"However, Speer being close with Hitler means that he might have biased opinions about Hitler's influences and how he became chancellor."

Good start: identifies potential bias. Missing: doesn't explain WHY — Speer's memoir was part of his post-Nuremberg self-rehabilitation. "Close with Hitler" is vague.

🔹 Level 3 (7-8/8)

"However, Source B's usefulness is limited by its provenance. Speer wrote this autobiography in 1970 — 38 years after the events and after serving a 20-year prison sentence at Nuremberg. His purpose was to rehabilitate his image by portraying himself as an apolitical technocrat rather than a committed Nazi. This means he may have selectively remembered or exaggerated Hitler's popularity to deflect responsibility. Nevertheless, as an insider who attended these rallies, his account of the crowds remains valuable when corroborated with other evidence."

Names the limitation precisely → Explains the purpose → Makes a balanced judgement.

🔸 Diya (Level 2+)

"However, the source provider isn't a first-hand witness, so the information is not 100% verified."

Problem: Ribbentrop WAS directly involved — he describes his own conversations. The real limitation is his Nazi bias, not distance from events.

🔹 Level 3 (7-8/8)

"Source C is useful because it is contemporary — written during the events of January 1933 — and provides an inside view of the backroom political negotiations between von Papen, Hindenburg, and the Nazis. However, its usefulness is limited by Ribbentrop's position as a committed Nazi supporter with a vested interest in presenting Hitler's appointment as inevitable and his own role as decisive. The diary may exaggerate his personal influence. A historian would need to corroborate his account with other sources from Hindenburg or von Papen's perspective."

🎯 Next Step: The NOP Framework

For every source utility question, analyse each source through three lenses:

LensQuestions to askThen say...
Nature What type of source is this? (diary, autobiography, speech, photograph, official document) "This is useful because [type] provides..."
Origin Who created it? When? What was their position? Contemporary or retrospective? "However, it is limited because [author] was..."
Purpose Why was it created? Who was the audience? What was the author trying to achieve? "The author's purpose was to... This affects reliability because..."

Golden rule: End with a judgement — "Overall, Source X is more useful than Source Y for this enquiry because..."
