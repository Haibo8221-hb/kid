AQA English Language Paper 1 Q4 — Graded Response

13/20 Level 3 · "Clear, relevant evaluation"
Date: 30 May 2026

Source Text

A little girl was sitting back on her heels beside a clump of daisies that grew against the fence. She had her back to Rosie and was holding tight to the handle of a large wicker basket that stood on the ground beside her. Cara seemed unfazed by the girl's presence and continued to move, engrossed, along the row of plants. Rosie bent forward to look through the clearest of the panes and peered closer. The child was small, maybe around eight or nine, although something in the tense hunch of her shoulders made her seem older. Her hair hung down her back in a matted, dusty-looking plait and she was wearing dressing-up clothes: an ankle-length dress and pinafore in washed-out greys and tans, like a home-made Cinderella costume.

Where on earth had she come from? She must be a neighbour's child but how had she got in? The wooden fences that separated the gardens between each of the houses in the terrace were high – surely too high for a child to climb.

The child glanced over her shoulder back towards the houses, a quick, furtive movement as if she were scanning the upper windows, afraid of being overlooked. Rosie caught a glimpse of her face, pale and drawn with anxiety, before the girl turned back and reached forward to quickly tuck a piece of trailing white cloth into the basket. Almost unconsciously, Rosie registered that the girl was left-handed like herself, and that there was something animal-like in her movements: quick, like the darting of a mouse or the flit of a sparrow, some small dun creature that moves fast to blend into the background.

Something wasn't right here. She had seen distress in those eyes.

The Question

Focus this part of your answer on the second part of the source, from line 24 to the end.

A student said, "I wasn't at all surprised by the disappearance of the stranger child at the end of the extract. The writer has left us in no doubt that she is just part of Rosie's imagination."

To what extent do you agree?

In your response, you could: · consider the disappearance of the stranger child
· evaluate how the writer presents the stranger child
· support your response with references to the text.

[20 marks]

Mostly

I partly agree on the student's statement of the stranger child disappearing and it being Rosie's imagination.

From line 26, Clara, Rosie's child is 'unfazed' by the girls presence. This indicates that she may not have seen the girl, as Rosie saw it. This further relates to the Student's Statement about Rosie imagining the Stranger Child. The questions from Rosie for the Child also connotes with the Student's Statement, 'Where on earth had she come from... how had she got in?' This suggests the unknown of the child and relates with Rosie's possible fantasy. 'Surely too high for a child to climb' Rosie questions the stranger child's ability, which instigates her superhuman appearance.

From line 36 and on words, the Stranger child is being described as fearful and fierce. The author's constant description of her appearance reinforces this idea. 'Afraid of being over looked' The child is frequently afraid showing that she might be guilty of something or is escaping from someone. This could oppose the Students Statement, as the stranger child might be fleeing from a danger.

However, the writer's description of the Super natural behaviour of the Stranger child contradicts with this assumption. 'Animal-like' presents the girl as a beastly figure almost non-human. This could further prove that she is Rosie's imagination rather than reality. 'Unconciously ... left handed like herself.' Rosie immediately assumes the girls dominant hand. This highlights her imagination as its the first thing she has thought of. She had seen 'distress in those eyes' Rosie's realisation of familiarity proves that she might be thinking about her real life situations rather than a real person. As the described Rosie's worries as a mother this assumption is further proven. The end of the extract, the author gave us a chillhanger, indicating the strong possibility of the girl's disappearance.

Examiner's Assessment

Strengths

  1. Clear argument structure — states position upfront ("Mostly... I partly agree"), then develops reasoning
  2. Uses textual evidence — embeds quotes from the passage ("unfazed", "animal-like", "distress in those eyes", "left-handed like herself")
  3. Considers both sides — presents imagination argument, then counters with "This could oppose the Student's Statement"
  4. Good structural awareness — identifies the cliffhanger ending and its effect

Areas to Improve

  1. Analyses WHAT not HOW — describes events without naming the writer's methods (simile, contrast, structural shifts)
  2. Vague phrasing — "contradicts with this assumption", "instigates her superhuman appearance" are unclear
  3. No alternative interpretations — doesn't explore whether the girl might be real (a runaway, a ghost, a memory)
  4. Missing method terminology — never uses words like "simile", "imagery", "tone", "perspective", "foreshadowing"

Diya vs Level 4 — Side by Side

🔸 Diya (Level 3 · 13/20)

'Animal-like' presents the girl as a beastly figure almost non-human. This could further prove that she is Rosie's imagination rather than reality.

Problem: States what the word means, not how the writer uses it as a method.

🔹 Level 4 (16+/20)

The writer's zoomorphic simile "animal-like" dehumanises the child, reducing her to something wild and untamed — a creature of Rosie's subconscious rather than reality. The cumulative effect of "darting... flit... small dun creature" creates a sense of something that exists only at the edge of perception. Alternatively, this imagery could symbolise Rosie's maternal anxiety — the child as vulnerable prey, something she instinctively wants to protect.

Why it works: Names the technique → analyses the effect → offers alternative reading → links back to the question.

🔸 Diya (Level 3)

The end of the extract, the author gave us a chillhanger, indicating the strong possibility of the girl's disappearance.

🔹 Level 4 (16+/20)

The writer's structural choice to end on a cliffhanger — the sudden, unexplained disappearance — mirrors the ambiguity of the child's existence throughout the extract. By refusing to resolve whether she was real or imagined, the writer forces the reader into Rosie's own uncertainty. This deliberate irresolution is the writer's central method: the extract is the question, and the ending is the answer — we cannot know, and neither can Rosie.

🎯 Next Step: The "Method First" Rule

For every paragraph in your next Q4 response, start with the writer's technique, not the plot:

❌ "The child is described as animal-like..."
✅ "The writer uses the zoomorphic simile 'animal-like' to..."

The 5-part paragraph formula:

  1. METHOD — "The writer uses [technique]..."
  2. QUOTE — embed it naturally: "...in the phrase 'animal-like'"
  3. EFFECT — "This dehumanises / creates / suggests..."
  4. ALTERNATIVE — "However, it could also imply..."
  5. LINK — "Therefore, the writer leaves us uncertain because..."
